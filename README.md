# Abstract

Poetry is traditionally investigated through literary interpretation, aesthetics, language, and cultural history, whereas thermodynamics and information theory examine the organization, transformation, and transmission of physical and statistical information. Despite their different explanatory domains, these disciplines share potentially productive questions concerning complexity, structure, memory, communication, and the emergence of organized patterns.

This article develops an interdisciplinary framework termed **Scientific Poetics through Farid Cosmology**, inspired by the Tanfarid Quantum Thermodynamic Universe (TQTU), to investigate how ecological experience and cultural knowledge may be organized into memorable poetic structures.

The study considers ten ecological and philosophical themes associated with the poetry of Padma Shri Haldhar Nag: The Old Banyan Tree, Soil (Mati), The River, The Farmer, Cremation Ground, Earthen Lamp, The Dove as Teacher, Cuckoo and Bulbul, Summer Rain, and Butterfly. These themes are examined in relation to ecological persistence, biogeochemical cycling, fluid transport, agricultural bioenergetics, mortality, communication, behavioural learning, acoustic information, hydrological renewal, and biological development.

The framework distinguishes two explanatory levels. The first employs established scientific concepts and measurable variables from thermodynamics, information theory, linguistics, cognitive psychology, and ecology. The second examines proposed TQTU constructs—including Chronons, Spin-Memory Fields, Farid Quantum Command Units, the Socio-Thermodynamic Tensor, the theta-field, and the Farid Gravity Law—as speculative theoretical interpretations requiring independent physical validation.

Three research constructs are developed: the **Normalized Poetic Entropic Gradient (PEG*)**, the **Cultural Memory Retention Index (CMRI)**, and the **Super Beauty Model**. These are intended to evaluate relationships among linguistic structure, semantic coherence, rhythmic organization, emotional resonance, cultural familiarity, and memory preservation.

A staged experimental protocol is proposed using authenticated poetic texts, computational linguistic analysis, reader-response assessments, delayed recall, and controlled physiological measurements.

The central hypothesis is that ecological poetry may preserve and transmit cultural knowledge through identifiable interactions among informational organization, cognitive processing, and socially meaningful imagery. Whether these relationships reflect the additional microscopic mechanisms proposed by TQTU remains an open scientific question.

# 1. Introduction: Poetry as an Organization of Human Experience

Human civilization has developed multiple methods for understanding reality.

Physical science investigates the measurable behaviour of matter, energy, fields, and interacting systems. Biological science examines living organisms and their environmental relationships. Cognitive science investigates perception, memory, language, and behaviour. Literature expresses the human experience of these processes through symbols, narratives, metaphors, and emotion.

These disciplines do not necessarily describe different worlds. They frequently investigate different aspects of the same world.

A river provides a useful example.

In physics, a river is a system of flowing water governed by conservation laws, gravitational potential, fluid dynamics, and interactions with its environment.

In ecology, the river transports nutrients, sediment, organisms, and energy through connected habitats.

In human culture, the same river may represent continuity, migration, nourishment, separation, or renewal.

A poem transforms selected aspects of these experiences into linguistic structures capable of being communicated and remembered.

This raises a fundamental interdisciplinary question:

**How does poetry transform complex environmental and social experience into structured cultural information that may persist across generations?**

The present study approaches this question through Scientific Poetics, a proposed research programme informed by information theory, cognitive science, ecological systems, and the conceptual perspective of Farid Cosmology.

The objective is not to declare poetry identical to physics. Rather, it is to determine which organizational relationships can be measured, which analogies are scientifically useful, and which proposed physical mechanisms require independent experimental testing.

# 3. Thermodynamics, Information, and Poetic Organization

## 3.1 Physical thermodynamic entropy

In statistical mechanics, the Boltzmann entropy of an equilibrium macrostate is expressed as:

\[
S_{\mathrm{th}}=k_B\ln\Omega
\]

where \(k_B\) is Boltzmann's constant and \(\Omega\) is the number of accessible microstates associated with the macrostate.

Physical entropy is measured in joules per kelvin.

For an open continuum system, a local entropy balance may be expressed as:

\[
\frac{\partial(\rho s)}{\partial t}
+\nabla\cdot(\rho s\mathbf v+\mathbf J_s)
=\sigma_s
\]

Here, \(\rho\) is mass density, \(s\) is specific entropy, \(\mathbf v\) is velocity, \(\mathbf J_s\) is non-advective entropy flux, and \(\sigma_s\) is local entropy production.

For conventional irreversible processes:

\[
\sigma_s\geq0
\]

This equation describes physical processes. It cannot be applied directly to poetry without defining the relevant physical variables and mechanisms.

## 3.2 Shannon information entropy

The uncertainty of a discrete random variable can be measured using Shannon entropy:

\[
H(X)=-\sum_i p_i\log_2p_i
\]

where \(p_i\) represents the probability of an outcome.

For linguistic research, the outcomes may represent words, phonemes, syllables, or other defined units.

Unlike thermodynamic entropy, Shannon entropy is measured in bits when logarithms use base 2.

The two concepts possess mathematical relationships in some physical contexts, but they are not interchangeable.

A poem with relatively low linguistic entropy is not necessarily more beautiful, meaningful, or scientifically informative than a poem with higher entropy.

Beauty may depend on a balance among predictability, novelty, emotional relevance, and comprehension.

# 4. Poetry as Information Transformation

The original Scientific Poetics proposal interprets poetic composition as the organization of complex experience into symbolic structure.

We represent this concept as:

\[
Y=\mathcal T(X;M,L,K)
\]

where:

- \(X\) represents environmental and social experience.
- \(Y\) represents the resulting poetic representation.
- \(M\) represents memory.
- \(L\) represents linguistic constraints.
- \(K\) represents cultural knowledge.
- \(\mathcal T\) represents the creative transformation.

This expression is a conceptual model rather than a fully specified physical equation.

Poetry does not preserve every detail of the experiences from which it emerges. Instead, it selects and organizes particular meanings.

A poem about a river may omit quantitative flow measurements while preserving emotionally significant information about migration, seasonal change, or community life.

This selective representation motivates the study of poetic information efficiency.

## 4.1 Semantic information efficiency

A candidate measure is:

\[
\eta_{\mathrm{poetic}}
=
\frac{I(Z;Y)}{L(Y)}
\]

where \(Z\) denotes predefined semantic features, \(I(Z;Y)\) denotes mutual information under a specified probability model, and \(L(Y)\) represents the poem's length.

This expression is meaningful only when the variables, sampling procedure, and probability distributions are clearly defined.

In practical studies, semantic fidelity may initially be estimated through independently scored comprehension and recall tasks rather than claiming that mutual information has been directly measured.

The resulting efficiency estimate should be interpreted as a task-dependent measure, not a universal measure of poetic value.

# 5. The Normalized Poetic Entropic Gradient

The original TQTU-inspired manuscript introduced the Poetic Entropic Gradient as the informational difference between experiential complexity and poetic organization.

A direct subtraction of entropy values is difficult to interpret when the two quantities arise from different linguistic models or probability spaces.

We therefore propose a restricted operational definition.

Let:

\[
h_{\mathrm{reference}}
\]

represent the estimated linguistic entropy rate of a topic-matched reference corpus, and:

\[
h_{\mathrm{poem}}
\]

represent the corresponding entropy rate of the poem.

Both must be estimated using the same language, tokenization scheme, and probabilistic model.

The normalized contrast is:

\[
\boxed{
PEG^*=
\frac{h_{\mathrm{reference}}-h_{\mathrm{poem}}}
{h_{\mathrm{reference}}+\epsilon}
}
\]

where \(\epsilon>0\) is a prespecified numerical stabilizer.

PEG* is dimensionless when the numerator and denominator use identical entropy-rate units.

A positive value indicates that the poem has a lower estimated entropy rate than the reference text under the selected model.

A negative value indicates the opposite.

Neither result establishes aesthetic superiority.

The term *gradient* is retained as a conceptual name. Mathematically, PEG* is a normalized entropy contrast rather than a spatial or temporal derivative.

## 5.1 Primary hypothesis

**H1:** PEG* contributes to the prediction of aesthetic response or memory retention after controlling for linguistic familiarity, text length, semantic coherence, and other relevant variables.

The hypothesis is rejected or revised if PEG* does not provide reproducible predictive improvement over the baseline model.

# 7. The Old Banyan Tree: Memory and Continuity

A mature banyan tree may survive across multiple human generations.

Its ecological persistence results from biological processes, including photosynthesis, respiration, growth, nutrient transport, and interactions with the surrounding environment.

Its cultural significance arises from the experiences that people associate with it.

A village tree may become connected with celebrations, conversations, childhood memories, marriages, departures, and mourning.

Two distinct quantities can therefore be considered:

\[
P_{\mathrm{ecological}}(t)
\]

representing the persistence of the physical organism, and:

\[
M_{\mathrm{cultural}}(t)
\]

representing the persistence of human memories associated with it.

The relationship between them is a possible subject for oral-history and cognitive-memory research.

### TQTU interpretation

Within Farid Cosmology, the banyan tree may be interpreted symbolically as a long-duration structure associated with repeated interactions.

The proposed Chronon concept interprets temporal progression through discrete events:

\[
t_n=n\tau_F
\]

where \(\tau_F\) is a hypothesized fundamental time interval.

This mathematical postulate has not been established experimentally. The discrete narrative events in a poem do not themselves provide evidence of quantized physical time.

Similarly, the tree may serve as a metaphor for a Spin-Memory archive, but biological persistence and social remembrance are not direct evidence that matter stores autobiographical information in a proposed universal field.

### Testable prediction

Narratives involving persistent ecological landmarks may improve the delayed recall of associated social events compared with matched narratives without such landmarks.

# 8. Soil (Mati): Matter Recycling and Ecological Humility

Soil connects geological, biological, chemical, and agricultural processes.

It contains mineral particles, organic matter, water, gases, microorganisms, and other living organisms.

Plants obtain water and mineral nutrients from soil, while decomposition returns many nutrients to ecological circulation.

For a specified material within a defined system boundary:

\[
\frac{dM}{dt}
=
\dot M_{\mathrm{in}}-\dot M_{\mathrm{out}}
\]

This expression represents material conservation.

The poetic interpretation of soil as origin, nourishment, and return can be examined in relation to these processes.

However, material recycling must not be confused with complete energy recycling. Biological systems require energy inputs and produce entropy.

### TQTU interpretation

Farid Cosmology emphasizes the physical dependence of organized systems on material and energetic interactions.

The soil theme provides a philosophical illustration of that dependence.

The Farid Gravity Law is a separate speculative physical proposal. The poem's imagery of belonging to the earth cannot establish its gravitational predictions.

### Testable prediction

Poems containing concrete bodily and ecological imagery may generate stronger reported sensorimotor imagery than abstract poems matched for length and difficulty.

# 9. The River: Flow, Continuity, and Identity

A river is a dynamic physical system.

Its water content, velocity, sediment load, and ecological conditions may change while its broader geographical identity persists.

Mass conservation is expressed through the continuity equation:

\[
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}
+
\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)=0
\]

For an incompressible fluid of constant density:

\[
\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0
\]

The equation describes fluid behaviour, not literary meaning.

Nevertheless, the river offers a powerful metaphor for continuity through change.

Human communities may similarly preserve recognizable traditions despite changes in their membership and circumstances.

### TQTU interpretation

The river illustrates the importance of gradients and transport in natural systems.

Its flow can be compared conceptually with other gradient-driven processes, including atmospheric circulation and plasma transport.

Such comparisons do not establish that all these processes share identical governing equations.

### Testable prediction

Ecological narratives involving familiar river environments may produce stronger comprehension or autobiographical associations among readers with direct experience of those environments.

# 14. Cuckoo and Bulbul: Acoustic Information and Aesthetic Experience

Bird vocalizations may perform biological functions related to mating, territorial behaviour, recognition, and communication.

Their acoustic structures can be measured using frequency, amplitude, temporal patterns, and repetition.

For an acoustic signal \(x(t)\), the Fourier transform is:

\[
X(f)=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
x(t)e^{-i2\pi ft}\,dt
\]

This provides a method for examining frequency content.

Poetic recitation can also be analysed acoustically, although human language and birdsong possess different biological and communicative functions.

### TQTU interpretation and the 432 Hz hypothesis

The proposed TQTU theta-field includes a hypothesis concerning biological responses to acoustic stimulation near 432 Hz.

There is currently no established evidence for a universal theta-field or a uniquely privileged 432 Hz resonance across biological systems.

A controlled experiment could compare natural birdsong, acoustically matched synthetic sounds, and appropriately designed frequency-manipulated conditions.

Sound pressure level, spectral characteristics, duration, and participant expectations should be controlled.

### Testable prediction

Natural vocalizations may produce different subjective and physiological responses from synthetic controls.

A specific 432 Hz mechanism would require additional evidence beyond a general preference for natural sounds.

# 15. Summer Rain: Hydrological Renewal

Rainfall is part of the hydrological cycle, involving evaporation, atmospheric transport, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, and runoff.

For a defined watershed, a simplified water balance may be written as:

\[
\Delta S=P-E-Q
\]

where \(S\) represents water storage, \(P\) precipitation, \(E\) evapotranspiration, and \(Q\) runoff over a specified interval, with other transfers assumed negligible.

Rainfall can replenish water resources, influence agricultural production, and alter ecological conditions.

In poetry, rain may represent relief, recovery, renewal, or continuity.

### TQTU interpretation

The hydrological cycle provides an example of an open physical system maintained by energy flows.

However, rainfall should not automatically be described as a reduction of total thermodynamic entropy.

Local cooling or improved ecological conditions do not imply that the total entropy production of the complete system is negative.

### Testable prediction

Readers familiar with seasonal agricultural environments may demonstrate stronger ecological comprehension and recall of rain-related poetry than readers without comparable experience, after controlling for language proficiency and education.

# 17. The Cultural Memory Retention Index

The proposed Cultural Memory Retention Index, or CMRI, is intended to quantify the retention of selected poetic information.

We define:

\[
\boxed{
CMRI(t)=
w_1R_s(t)+w_2R_e(t)+w_3R_c(t)
}
\]

where:

- \(R_s(t)\) is the proportion of predefined semantic themes correctly recalled at time \(t\).
- \(R_e(t)\) is the accuracy of ecological-concept recognition.
- \(R_c(t)\) is the accuracy of culturally relevant association recognition.

The weights satisfy:

\[
w_1+w_2+w_3=1,\qquad w_i\geq0
\]

Each component is normalized between 0 and 1.

The index is a proposed measurement instrument and must undergo reliability and validity testing.

## 17.1 Memory decay model

A candidate model for delayed retention is:

\[
\boxed{
CMRI(t)=C_\infty+
(C_0-C_\infty)e^{-\mu t}
}
\]

where:

- \(C_0\) represents initial retention.
- \(C_\infty\) represents an estimated long-term asymptote.
- \(\mu\) is a fitted decay-rate parameter.
- \(t\) is elapsed time.

The model assumes an exponential approach toward an asymptote.

Human memory may follow more complex trajectories. Power-law and alternative forgetting models should therefore be compared.

The decay coefficient is a behavioural measurement, not direct evidence of a physical Spin-Memory Field.

# 20. Experimental Research Programme

## Stage I: Corpus authentication

Obtain the original-language texts and reliable published translations of the selected poems.

For each poem, record:

- Original title and language.
- Published collection and edition.
- Translator, where applicable.
- Publication year and page numbers.
- Textual variations between editions.

## Stage II: Computational analysis

Measure:

- Word and syllable frequencies.
- Conditional linguistic entropy.
- Rhythmic and phonological repetition.
- Semantic coherence.
- Topic-related vocabulary.
- PEG* relative to matched reference texts.

All models, preprocessing choices, and exclusion rules should be documented.

## Stage III: Cognitive evaluation

Recruit participants with recorded language proficiency, cultural familiarity, and relevant demographic characteristics.

Collect:

- Aesthetic ratings.
- Emotional resonance ratings.
- Comprehension scores.
- Ecological relevance ratings.
- Immediate recall.
- Delayed recall.

Sample sizes should be determined using an appropriate power analysis.

## Stage IV: Model comparison

Compare three candidate models.

**M0 — Baseline:** Text length, language proficiency, cultural familiarity, and participant effects.

**M1 — Information model:** Baseline variables plus linguistic entropy and PEG*.

**M2 — Super Beauty model:** M1 plus semantic coherence, rhythm, meaningful novelty, emotional resonance, and memory-related variables.

Use held-out prediction or appropriately structured cross-validation to evaluate model performance.

Where multiple ratings come from the same participant or poem, the validation procedure must prevent information leakage.

## Stage V: Physiological investigations

If feasible, investigate physiological responses during poetry recitation or controlled acoustic exposure.

Possible measures include heart-rate variability, electroencephalography, and subjective relaxation.

These measures require validated recording procedures and appropriate controls.

Physiological correlations must not automatically be interpreted as evidence of TQTU-specific microscopic mechanisms.

# 21. Statistical Testing of φ-Scaling and 1/f Patterns

The original TQTU-inspired proposal includes exploratory analyses of golden-ratio relationships and scale-dependent patterns.

These may be investigated as secondary hypotheses.

For φ-scaling:

\[
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}
\approx1.618
\]

Potential measurements include line-length ratios, stanza proportions, and predefined structural divisions.

However, a statistically meaningful test requires a justified tolerance range and null distributions generated from appropriate matched texts.

For spectral analysis:

\[
P(f)\propto f^{-\beta}
\]

A value of \(\beta\) near 1 may indicate an approximately 1/f spectral relationship over a specified frequency range.

Such findings require adequate data length, justified frequency ranges, uncertainty estimates, and comparisons against suitable null models.

Neither φ-like ratios nor 1/f patterns, if observed, would independently establish TQTU.

# 22. Ethical Considerations

Research involving human participants should receive approval from an appropriate independent ethics committee before recruitment.

Participants should provide informed consent in a language they understand.

Data collection should minimize unnecessary personal information.

Audio recordings, cognitive test results, and physiological measurements should be stored securely.

Participants should be permitted to withdraw according to the approved protocol.

Studies involving children require additional safeguards, including parental permission and age-appropriate assent.

Copyright and translation permissions must also be respected when digitizing and analysing published poetry.

# 23. Limitations

This study has several important limitations.

First, its present form is theoretical and interpretative. No new participant data or computational corpus results are reported.

Second, the English working titles and textual sources of the ten poems require complete bibliographic documentation before publication.

Third, linguistic entropy is sensitive to tokenization, language models, corpus selection, and translation.

Fourth, cultural familiarity may strongly influence aesthetic ratings and memory performance.

Fifth, the proposed PEG*, CMRI, and Super Beauty measures require independent validation.

Sixth, the TQTU-specific physical constructs remain unvalidated. Successful literary or cognitive predictions would not, by themselves, establish their microscopic physical interpretation.

Finally, aesthetic appreciation is culturally and individually variable. No universal mathematical law of beauty is assumed.

The butterfly represents developmental transformation.

Together, these themes offer a conceptual model of ecological and cultural existence.

Their importance lies not in proving that all systems obey a single newly discovered physical law, but in making their relationships available for systematic interdisciplinary investigation.

Farid Cosmology provides a broader theoretical motivation for seeking cross-scale organizational relationships.

Scientific Poetics provides a more immediate empirical pathway through which selected relationships can be measured and evaluated.

# 25. Conclusion

Scientific Poetics through Farid Cosmology proposes an interdisciplinary approach to the study of ecological poetry, cultural memory, and aesthetic organization.

Using ten poetic themes associated with Haldhar Nag, the study examines how human experiences of environmental persistence, material cycling, biological energy transformation, mortality, communication, and renewal may be represented through literary structures.

The research distinguishes established physical and informational principles from speculative TQTU-specific mechanisms.

Three proposed constructs—the Normalized Poetic Entropic Gradient, Cultural Memory Retention Index, and Super Beauty Model—provide candidate tools for investigating linguistic organization, semantic preservation, aesthetic response, and delayed recall.

The framework does not claim that poetic beauty has already been reduced to thermodynamic law.

Instead, it proposes a measurable scientific question:

**Can the enduring cultural and aesthetic significance of poetry be partly explained through identifiable relationships among information structure, meaningful novelty, emotional resonance, ecological familiarity, and human memory?**

Answering this question requires authenticated texts, reproducible computational analysis, controlled reader studies, and independent evaluation.

The broader TQTU hypothesis—that such organizational relationships ultimately arise from deeper universal physical mechanisms—remains a separate proposition requiring distinctive physical predictions and experimental evidence.

Scientific Poetics therefore offers both a literary interpretation and a proposed research programme, preserving the creative insight of Farid Cosmology while subjecting its scientific claims to empirical scrutiny.

**Poetry may be understood as one of humanity's culturally evolved methods for organizing experience, preserving selected meanings, and transmitting ecological knowledge through time.**
